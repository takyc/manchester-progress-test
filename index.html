A 67 year old man presents with a 30 second episode of syncope after playing padel with his colleagues. He reports no prodromal symptoms and recovered rapidly. He can not identify a trigger for the episode.

What is the most likely cause of his syncope?

Correct: Cardiogenic Syncope

This is most likely cardiogenic syncope due to the short duration, lack of prodromal symptoms and rapid recovery. He likely has a bradyarrhythmia or a structural heart abnormality such as aortic stenosis which would not be uncommon in his age group. Additionally this is in the context of exercise (although calling Padel an exercise is a push)

Vasovagal syncope is also a good differential. However this would normally involve a prodrome of light-headedness and visual changes, as well as a clear trigger such as a stressful/emotional event or a long period of standing (instead of exertion).

Transient Ischemic Attacks will present with stroke symptoms such as facial drooping, limb weakness, slurred speech and visual changes. Although these symptoms would fully recover in 24 hours, the recovery would not be as rapid as cardiogenic syncope.

A Focal Impaired Awareness Seizure could fit in this time frame. However a post ictal period of confusion and fatigue is typical and there is no collateral history of repetitive movements. The preceding exercise also points towards a diagnosis of cardiogenic syncope.

Hypoglycaemia is unlikely as prodromal symptoms of shakiness, sweating and confusion. This most often occurs in diabetics who have skipped meals or taken too much insulin. Symptoms take much longer than half a minute to resolve without intervention.

Correct Answer: Fluconazole 150 mg

Fluconazole is correct because this itching and discharge is typical of Vaginal Candidiasis. This is a fungal infection due to Candida Albicans, producing a classic cheese/curd-like white discharge that is odourless.

Metronidazole is a treatment for Trichomonas Vaginalis. This presents with a “musty” smelling yellow/green purulent discharge and a classic finding is a “strawberry cervix”. It can also be used to treat bacterial vaginosis, which presents with white/gray discharge that would not be odourless or itchy.

Oral prednisolone is not a standard treatment for vaginal discharge

IM Benzylpenicillin is a treatment for Syphilis, which primarily presents as single painless ulcer known as a “chancre”

Ceftriaxone is a treatment for Neisseria Gonorrhoea. This presents with a thick yellow discharge with a potential odour

The hyperuricaemia, hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia and hyperphosphatemia is pathognomonic with tumour lysis syndrome (although technically we need to compare to his baseline). A 12 year old boy presents with a painful limp over the last month. The child complains of a dull ache over the hip, is in the 98th centile weight for his age and denies trauma. Upon examination, he has a loss of internal rotation of the leg during hip flexion.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct Answer: Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis

Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis is correct. This is where there is a slip in the growth plate between the head and neck of the femur (This is not a hip dislocation). Often occurs in obese boys aged 12-14. This causes pain and a loss of internal rotation of the leg in hip flexion. SCFE requires urgent surgical treatment to prevent further slipping.

Perthes disease is idiopathic avascular necrosis of the femoral head, affecting children aged 4-8, most commonly in boys.

Septic arthritis would present with an acutely unwell patient (fever, immobility of joint, drop in BP, tachycardia)

Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis is arthritis in a child under 16. This would present with persistent joint pain, stiffness, swelling and warmth worse after resting. Associated symptoms include fever, rash and uveitis.

Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip is a congenital disorder where the hip joint is not held properly due to a shallow acetabulum and loose hip joint ligaments. Most common in girls who were born in breech presentation, and would’ve presented at a far younger age (within 6 months, rarely present up to 3 years old)

A 36 year old patient is clinically dry after a 7 day history of persistent vomiting and diarrhoea. 24 hours ago they had a blood pressure of 93/58 mmHg and a Sodium of 159 mmol/L (135-145 mmol/L). They are given 0.18% Sodium Chloride with 4% glucose. They undergo a seizure.

What is the most likely mechanism behind this process?

Correct Answer: Cerebral Oedema

0.18% Sodium Chloride is a hypotonic solution. If given at a rate too high, water can enter brain tissue via osmosis causing oedema. This increases intracranial pressure causing seizures, comas and death.

Hypovolemic shock can cause seizures, but the patient is undergoing active treatment for this.

Diabetic Ketoacidosis can also cause seizures. The most common cause of death from DKA is cerebral oedema. However, this vignette describes a 7 day history (normally DKA is within hours) and no evidence of T1DM.

Pontine Demyelination occurs the other way around, during the rapid correction of hyponatremia. Typically presents as severe neurological decline several days after correction. A useful phrase to remember for sodium correction is high to low - your brain will blow, low to high - your pons will die.

Aseptic Meningitis would present with a fever, headache, light sensitivity and stiff neck. Infective origin (viral) instead of iatrogenic.

An 85 year old woman has trouble walking downstairs due to double vision. She has no trouble walking up stairs. Her head is tilted at an axis when speaking to you.

Which nerve is most likely affected?

Correct Answer: Trochlear Nerve

Trouble walking downstairs due to diplopia is classic for trochlear nerve palsy. CN IV innervates the superior oblique muscle which is responsible for depressing and intorting the eye. This subsequently causes diplopia worse on downgaze and a compensatory head tilt.

Optic Nerve damage can present as optic neuritis, such as in MS. This involves reduced colour vision, visual acuity and eye pain.

Oculomotor nerve palsy causes the eye to be “down and out” as the lateral rectus and superior oblique muscle remain intact.

Ophthalmic nerve is a branch of the trigeminal nerve. Damage to this nerve is normally due to compression, causing severe shock/stabbing pain - trigeminal neuralgia. Can also be damaged by varicella zoster virus reactivation.

Abducens nerve damage leads to issues abducting the eye and horizontal diplopia instead of vertical

Correct Answer: Immediate Referral to Paediatrics

This needs to be urgently referred due to indication of malignancy – most likely acute lymphoblastic leukaemia. Any child with unexplained hepatosplenomegaly or petechiae must be urgently referred for this reason.

A 59 year old man has an INR of 0.8 during routine warfarin monitoring. He has a mechanical heart valve in place for severe aortic stenosis. His INR is normally within target range (2.5-3.5) but he has recently started a new medication.

Which of the following could be the cause of his newly reduced INR?

Correct Answer: Carbamazepine

Carbamazepine is an antiepileptic that is a potent CYP inducer. Inducing the CYP enzyme will increases the metabolism of CYP metabolised warfarin, decreasing its half-life and subsequently decreasing the INR (Calculated by Prothrombin Time) leading to insufficient anticoagulation.

Niacin is a CYP inhibitor and will increase the INR by increasing warfarin’s half-life.

Clarithromycin is also a CYP inhibitor and increase the INR

Lisinopril is CYP independent and is safe to take alongside warfarin

Gabapentin is also CYP independent and is safe to take alongside warfarin

An 83 year old man presents with a 2 month history of a red rash along his arms, thighs and abdomen. He says the rashes are itchy and form blisters but do not break when scratched. He denies taking any new medications, pets or travel. He is systemically well. The doctor prescribes corticosteroids which rapidly improve his condition.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct Answer: Bullous Pemphigoid

This is Bullous Pemphigoid, an autoimmune condition primarily affecting the elderly causing pruritic, tense bullae across the extremities and trunk. It occurs due to autoantibodies targeting hemidesmosomes and responds well to corticosteroids.

Atopic Dermatitis is eczema, more common in children and adults instead of the elderly. Although it presents with a pruritic red rash, it typically affects skin creases and is associated with dryness more than blisters.

Urticaria is a pruritic raised rash that do not last past 24 hours, normally caused by a trigger such as food medication or an infection.

Pemphigus Vulgaris is also an autoimmune condition similar to bullous pemphigoid, but the blisters easily rupture and the mucous membranes are affected. There is positive Nikolsky sign and the autoimmune antibodies target desmogleins. Treatment of choice is high dose corticosteroids.

Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis is a life threatening skin reaction where >30% of the skin detaches from the mucous membranes. The skin would peel off at touch (Positive Nikolsky sign) and the patient would be severely systemically unwell

A 36 year old woman presents with fatigue and palpitations. She mentions recent diarrhoea and heat intolerance. There is visible fine tremor and a goitre which is painful upon touch. She had a viral upper respiratory infection 3 weeks ago.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct Answer: De Quervain’s Thyroiditis

Fatigue, palpitations, heat intolerance and diarrhoea all indicate hyperthyroidism. The tender goitre and recent viral URTI reveal that the cause of her hyperthyroidism is De Quervain’s thyroiditis. This is a self limiting inflammation of the thyroid gland after a viral infection leading to a hyperthyroid phase, followed by a hypothyroid phase, followed by full recovery.

Sick Euthyroid Syndrome is where the thyroid gland is functioning normally but T3 and T4 appear low on testing, presenting with hypothyroid symptoms. It is caused by severe illness/stress leading to the normal functioning thyroid gland producing less hormone.

Hashimoto’s disease is an autoimmune thyroiditis which causes hypothyroidism not hyperthyroidism, and the goitre is painless

Graves’ disease is an autoimmune disorder which although causes hyperthyroidism, the goitre would be painless. Other pathognomonic features such as exophthalmos would also be present

Toxic Multinodular Goitre is less likely due to the history of viral infection and no mention of an irregular goitre. Also the goitre tends to be painless.

A 15 year old girl presents with a 2 day history of severe right lower quadrant pain. She has lost her appetite. She has a history of recurrent tonsillitis with her last episode being 7 days ago.

Which of the following diagnoses is most likely?

Correct Answer: Mesenteric Adenitis

The correct answer is Mesenteric Adenitis. Mesenteric Adenitis is the transient, self-limiting inflammation of the lymph nodes in the mesentery that can present as RLQ and umbilical pain after a respiratory infection, commonly in children and teens.

Appendicitis is often mimicked by mesenteric adenitis. However, the mention of tonsilitis makes mesenteric adenitis more likely. Additionally, most cases will mention pain beginning in the umbilical region.

Ovarian torsion is an important differential but simply rarer and presents more acutely, typically in those in their 20s-30s. Risk factors of an enlarged ovary (e.g. PCOS or ovarian cyst) will normally be mentioned. Confirmed via imaging with Transvaginal Ultrasound.

Ectopic Pregnancy is an important cause to rule out. It can present in a variety of ways but in the history they will normally mention a missed or “late” period. An hCG should be done to rule this out but it is still epidemiologically less likely than mesenteric adenitis in this context.

Renal Colic typically presents in older patients (30s-60s) with a more acute timeline of 10/10 pain. Risk factors such as a low fluid intake and a high protein diet would often be mentioned.

Correct Answer: Patellar Tendon

Patellar Tendon is the correct answer as this is the only structure that would compromise the extensor mechanism. Patellar Tendon Rupture is an uncommon knee injury, usually damaged in jumping.

Achilles Tendon Rupture would not present with knee pain

ACL injury normally occurs from a sudden change in direction on a firmly planted foot. Typical examination findings would be excess forward translation of the tibia, tested via Lachman’s and anterior drawer.

Iliotibial band damage tends to have a gradual onset due to overuse (in endurance athletes). Knee pain would typically be lateral.

A 22 year old woman loses consciousness for 2 minutes after skiing into a tree. She is urgently airlifted via helicopter to a nearby hospital where she receives a CT non-contrast which appears normal and she has marked neurological improvement.

Which injury is the reason a follow-up CT is recommended?

Correct Answer: Epidural Hematoma

This vignette describes the timeline of an epidural/extradural hematoma. This is because the loss of consciousness is followed by a “lucid interval”. Initial CT may appear normal, but the middle meningeal artery could still be bleeding and slowly cause increased ICP, coma and death due to mass effect. The second CT would show a lens-shaped hyperdensity. Emergency Craniotomy may be indicated.

Subdural hematoma is typically in elderly patients following mild head injury, due to a bridging vein rupture. Its timeline is more delayed and insidious and CT would show a banana shaped hyperdensity

An SAH presents with a sudden headache that peaks < 5 mins at a severity described as the worst of their life. If suspected, a 12 hour LP is normally ordered instead of a follow-up CT.

ICH is often spontaneous instead of being caused by trauma. Important risk factors include hypertension, vascular malformations and increasing age.

Intraventricular haemorrhage primarily occurs in premature infants, although it can occur as an extension of an ICH.

An 8 year old boy is brought to the GP by his father concerned that he is “acting strange”. His father describes repetitive head jerks and squinting alongside sniffling on most days over the past year, seeming to occur at random but worst during periods of stress. The boy claims he is trying to suppress these movements but seems relieved after they occur. Neurological exam is normal, besides the head jerks and frequent throat clearing. He is otherwise well.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct Answer: Tourette’s Syndrome

The description of involuntary, repetitive, uncomfortable tics for an extensive period of time, which also bring relief, is indicative of Tourette’s syndrome. It is more common in males and begins at a young age (around 5)

OCD involves obsessions and compulsions and typically presents older. Although these movements could be argued as acts on intrusive thoughts, they fit better with tics, being both motor and vocal (sniffling and throat clearing).

ADHD is characterised by inattention. The repetitive nature of the tics align better with Tourette’s. There is a strong association of ADHD with Tourette’s (60%).

Infantile Spasms aka West Syndrome occurs much younger (3-8 months) who have “salaam attacks” which are seizures that cause babies to flex and extend their back and arms in synchrony. It carries a poor prognosis.

Cerebral Palsy would typically present earlier when babies and toddlers don’t hit their gross motor developmental milestones, such as failing to sit at 8 months, crawl by 10, or walk by 18. Neurological examination would find abnormal tone, hyperreflexia and a positive Babinski sign.

A 61 year old man presents to A&E with nausea, vomiting and severe abdominal pain. An A-E assessment shows Kussmaul breathing with a BP of 95/58 mmHg, blood glucose of 21.1mmol/l and pH of 7.21. He is treated immediately with IV saline and an insulin infusion, but subsequently falls into ventricular fibrillation. He was successfully defibrillated and the underlying cause was treated.

What was the cause of the cardiac event?

Correct Answer: Hypokalaemia

The correct answer is hypokalaemia, secondary to administration of insulin which drives potassium back into cells via the Na+/K+ pump. This causes a prolonged QT that can lead to ventricular arrhythmias. Due to this risk, K+ must be monitored regularly, and potassium should be given in fluids with caution after the first litre of saline.

A myocardial infarction is unlikely to have occurred as there is no causative event or mention of atherosclerosis.

Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation can occur in DKA, but would present with bleeding and would not be the cause of VFib

Although a PE could cause cardiac arrest, there are no risk factors

Hypoglycaemia may occur after insulin therapy. Once blood glucose drops below 14mmol/l, 10% dextrose should be started to prevent hypoglycaemia. Hypoglycaemia would be a rare cause of cardiac arrest.

A 27 year old woman requests contraception. She has past medical history of large fibroids and migraines with aura.

Which of the following is the most appropriate method of contraception for her?

Correct Answer: Progesterone-only Pill (POP)

The POP is the most appropriate due to the other methods being contraindicated. Other cases where the POP is firstline include breastfeeding women.

The COCP is contraindicated by migraines with aura.

The IUS is contraindicated due to structural distortion of the uterine cavity from large fibroids.

The IUD is contraindicated due to structural distortion of the uterine cavity from large fibroids.

The Contraceptive Patch is contraindicated by migraines with aura.

A 65 year old male presents with a 2 day history of visual problems. He describes a “dark curtain” covering the top of his vision. He denies any pain or trauma.

Which of the following is the most likely risk factor for his presentation?

Correct Answer: Myopia

The painless loss of vision coming down “like a curtain” is highly buzzy for retinal detachment, when the neurosensory retina separates from the underlying retinal pigment epithelium. High myopia increases risk of detachment due to elongation of the eyeball which stretches the retina. Other risk factors include trauma and eye surgery.

Tetracyclines can cause idiopathic intracranial hypertension that can affect vision. However this presents as a headache. Tetracyclines also causes skin photosensitivity

Alport syndrome is a genetic disorder affecting type IV collagen. This causes other ocular abnormalities such as anterior lenticonus (bulging lens) and would present at a younger age. It would also cause nephritis and sensorineural hearing loss.

Presbyopia is age related worsening of near vision. It is not associated with retinal detachment.

Multiple sclerosis is associated with optic neuritis and presents with painful vision loss, reduced visual acuity and reduced colour vision.
